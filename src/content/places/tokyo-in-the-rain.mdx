It rained for three days straight in Tokyo. Most tourists complained. I thought it was the best thing that could have happened.

Rain transforms Tokyo. The neon signs reflect off wet pavement and suddenly every street becomes a river of colour. The crowds thin out. The city reveals its quieter self.

> Some cities become more beautiful when everyone goes home.

## Shinjuku at night

There's a back alley in Shinjuku — Golden Gai — that feels like stepping into someone's memory of the 1960s. Tiny bars, each seating maybe six people. In the rain, the lanterns outside each door glow a little warmer.

I found a bar run by a woman who spoke no English. She poured me whisky and we communicated entirely through gestures and laughter. It was one of the best conversations I've ever had.

## The temple in the morning

Meiji Shrine in the rain is something everyone should experience. The gravel paths are empty. The trees drip. The torii gates look like they're holding up the sky.

I stood under the main gate for twenty minutes, watching the rain fall on ancient stones. Nobody asked me to move. Nobody was there to ask.

## What Tokyo teaches

Japan understands something that most cultures don't: beauty exists in impermanence. The rain will stop. The cherry blossoms will fall. The moment you're in will never happen again.

That's not a reason to be sad. It's a reason to pay attention.
