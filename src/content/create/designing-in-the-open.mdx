I started sharing my design work mid-process. Not the polished final versions — the messy, uncertain, half-formed stages where nothing looks right yet.

It was terrifying. And it was the best creative decision I've made.

> The finished product tells people what you made. The process tells them how you think.

## Why it works

When you share finished work, people judge the result. When you share work-in-progress, people engage with the thinking. They offer suggestions. They point out things you missed. They become invested in the outcome.

The work gets better because more minds are touching it. And more importantly, you get better because you're forced to articulate your decisions before they're final.

## The fear

Yes, people see your mistakes. Yes, some ideas look terrible in their early stages. But here's what I've learned: vulnerability is the price of connection. If you only show people the highlight reel, they'll admire you but never trust you.

Trust comes from honesty. And honesty means showing the ugly drafts alongside the beautiful finals.

## The practice

Every week, I post three things: something I'm working on, something that isn't working, and something I learned. It takes ten minutes. The feedback is worth hours of solitary thinking.

Try it. Your creative work will thank you.
