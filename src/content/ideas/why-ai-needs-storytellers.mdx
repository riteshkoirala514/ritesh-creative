Everyone is talking about what AI can do. Almost nobody is talking about what it means.

That's a problem. Not because the technology isn't impressive — it is. But because the people building it and the people using it are having completely different conversations.

> Technology without narrative is just machinery. Stories are how humans make sense of change.

## The gap

Engineers think in capabilities. Users think in outcomes. And right now, there's a massive gap between "this model can process 128K tokens" and "this tool can help you write a letter to your mother."

The companies that will win the AI era aren't the ones with the best models. They're the ones with the best storytellers — people who can translate capability into meaning.

## What storytellers bring

A good storyteller asks different questions than a good engineer. Not "what can this do?" but "why would anyone care?" Not "how does this work?" but "how does this feel?"

These aren't soft questions. They're the hardest questions in business.

## The opportunity

If you can write clearly, think about human needs, and understand technology well enough to know what's possible — you are exactly the person this moment needs.

AI doesn't need more builders right now. It needs more translators. People who can stand between the technology and the world and say: "Here's what this actually means for your life."

That's the most valuable skill of the next decade.
